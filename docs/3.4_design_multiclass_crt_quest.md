# TreeGov — Phase 3.4 design: multiclass C&RT and QUEST

**Purpose.** This document defines the design of Phase 3.4 (multiclass support in C&RT and QUEST) before any code is written. It is the reference for the implementation sub-phases 3.4.a and 3.4.b, and the audit trail for the design decisions taken on 12/10/2026.

**Audience.** The project author, future maintainers, and any MRM reviewer who needs to understand how multiclass targets are handled in the two engines that Phase 3.1 left binary.

**Status.** Draft — pending audit by the project author before implementation begins. Sections marked [PROPOSED] contain proposals that require explicit approval.

**Companion documents.**
- `docs/3.1_design_multiclass.md` — Phase 3.1 design (multiclass in CHAID). This document mirrors its structure.
- `docs/3.3_design_population_categories.md` — Phase 3.3 design (population categories). Same structure.
- `docs/design_decisions.md` — implementation decisions across the project.
- `docs/methodology.md` — mathematical formulas with citations.

## §0. Scope and objective

### 0.1 What 3.4 delivers

Phase 3.4 opens the two remaining engines (C&RT and QUEST) to targets with K ≥ 3 classes. Today only CHAID and CHAID_EXHAUSTIVE accept K > 2; C&RT and QUEST raise `ValueError` from `Session.train` since Phase 3.1.c.

Specifically:

- **C&RT** gains the ability to build a tree on K ≥ 3 classes. The Gini impurity already supports K classes by construction; the only change is lifting the block imposed in `Session.train`.
- **QUEST** gains multiclass Stage 1 and Stage 2. Both stages are binary by canonical formulation (Loh & Shih 1997); two documented extensions are proposed (§3).

### 0.2 What 3.4 does NOT deliver

- **Multiclass metrics** (multiclass Gini, KS, HL, confusion matrix K×K). Deferred to Phase 3.5.
- **Multiclass reports** (gains table, classification matrix). Deferred to Phase 3.5.
- **Multiclass stability** (PSI/CSI stay target-agnostic, but the Gini Drop check remains binary-only, as in 3.1).
- **Cost-complexity pruning on K > 2 trees.** The current pruning uses `r(t) = n - max(n_events)`, which generalises to K classes. The design documents this, but no new pruning tests are added in 3.4.

### 0.3 Why this scope

The scope is deliberately narrow: two engines, one conceptual extension each, tested against the Phase 3.1 parity invariant.

## §1. Current state

### 1.1 The block imposed by Phase 3.1.c

`Session.train` raises `ValueError` when the engine is not CHAID and the target has K > 2:

    if K > 2 and engine_name not in ("CHAID", "CHAID_EXHAUSTIVE"):
        raise ValueError(
            f"[session] engine {engine_name!r} no soporta targets "
            f"multiclase en Fase 3.1 (K={K} clases detectadas). "
            f"Use CHAID o CHAID_EXHAUSTIVE para multiclase, o espere "
            f"a la Fase 3.4."
        )

### 1.2 QUEST internal block

`QuestEngine._prepare` raises `ValueError` if it detects more than 2 classes in the target. This block is independent of `Session.train`.

### 1.3 C&RT internal state

`CrtEngine.evaluate` computes Gini impurity with `1 - sum(p_k^2)` for any K. There is no K-dependent code path in C&RT other than the fast-path assumption that `pd.factorize` returns at most 2 classes. The engine, in isolation, already works with K ≥ 3.

## §2. C&RT multiclass — the small sub-phase

### 2.1 No engine change

The Gini impurity formula `I(t) = 1 - sum_k p_k^2` is defined for any K. The subset search, the threshold sweep, the surrogate logic, and the impurity gain all operate over the K-length class vector. No arithmetic change is needed.

### 2.2 Surrogate agreement is binary by construction

The agreement between a primary and a candidate split is the fraction of rows where the two binary orientations match. It does not depend on K. The embedded `SplitConfig` of a surrogate is evaluated with the same Gini gain used by the parent node.

### 2.3 The only change

`Session.train` raises the block when the engine is `CRT` and K > 2. The change is: remove `CRT` from the blocked set, leaving QUEST blocked until sub-phase 3.4.b.

### 2.4 Sub-phase 3.4.a

A single commit that:
1. Removes C&RT from the block in `Session.train`.
2. Adds a test `test_train_allows_crt_multiclass` in `tests/test_session_state.py`.
3. Adds a test `test_crt_multiclass_parity` that verifies a K=3 C&RT tree builds and produces coherent PD vectors (summing to 1 per node).
4. Updates the audit trail.

No engine change. No fingerprint change. No downstream change.

## §3. QUEST multiclass — the substantial sub-phase

### 3.1 Stage 1 with K classes — [PROPOSED]

Canonical QUEST Stage 1 (Loh & Shih 1997) uses an ANOVA F-test with two groups (the two target classes) for continuous/ordinal predictors, and a Pearson χ² on a c×2 table for nominal predictors.

**Proposed extension.** Replace the two-group F-test with a K-group F-test:

    F = (SSB / (K - 1)) / (SSW / (N - K))

where SSB and SSW are the between- and within-group sums of squares over the K target classes. For K = 2 this reduces exactly to the current binary F. The nominal χ² is unchanged in form (it already handles K columns).

**Justification.** This is the natural generalisation of the F-test. It preserves the interpretation of "the predictor discriminates between target classes", without introducing new statistical machinery.

**Alternatives considered and rejected.**
- One-vs-rest: K separate binary F-tests, take the minimum p-value. Rejected: multiplies the multiple-testing surface and breaks the Bonferroni logic.
- χ² for all predictors regardless of continuity: rejected, discards the ordinal/continuous structure of the predictor.

### 3.2 Stage 2 with K classes — [PROPOSED]

Canonical Stage 2 uses univariate QDA: find the root of the quadratic decision boundary between the two Gaussian class-conditional densities. This is intrinsically binary.

**Proposed extension.** Replace Stage 2 with a **Gini reduction sweep** over the selected predictor:

- Continuous/ordinal: sweep over midpoints between consecutive distinct values, choose the cut that maximises ΔI = I(parent) − (n_L/N)·I(L) − (n_R/N)·I(R) with Gini over K classes.
- Nominal with K ≤ 10: exhaustive over 2^(K−1) − 1 binary partitions, as C&RT does.
- Nominal with K > 10: rate-based heuristic, as C&RT does.

**Justification.** Stage 2 becomes a "find the best binary cut for K classes" step, identical to what C&RT does. This is coherent with the tree builder's expectations (binary split per node, K-length PD vector) and does not require multivariate machinery.

**Alternatives considered and rejected.**
- Multivariate QDA (K × K covariance matrices): rejected for now. Introduces new mathematics not covered by `docs/methodology.md` and breaks the univariate-split promise of QUEST (each split is a single threshold or a category subset).
- One-vs-rest QDA: rejected. Breaks the single-cut-per-node promise; a node would need K splits.

**Fidelity note.** This is a documented deviation from canonical Loh & Shih (1997), which is strictly binary. The deviation is deliberate and necessary to open QUEST to K > 2. The justification anchors on internal coherence with C&RT and on preserving the binary nature of each node's split.

### 3.3 Brown-Forsythe tie-breaker with K classes

The canonical tie-breaker uses Brown-Forsythe on the two class groups. With K classes, it uses the K-group Brown-Forsythe (the same generalisation as §3.1, applied to Levene's z-scores). For K = 2 this reduces exactly to the current behaviour.

### 3.4 Sub-phase 3.4.b

A commit that:
1. Extends `QuestEngine._prepare` to accept K > 2 targets (remove the internal block).
2. Extends Stage 1 with the K-group F-test.
3. Extends Stage 1 tie-breaker with the K-group Brown-Forsythe.
4. Replaces Stage 2 QDA with the Gini reduction sweep for K > 2 (keep the QDA path for K = 2, ensuring parity).
5. Removes QUEST from the block in `Session.train`.
6. Adds tests: multiclass Stage 1, Stage 2, end-to-end build, and K=2 parity.
7. Updates the audit trail and `docs/methodology.md` (a new §3.8 subsection for the multiclass extensions).

Fingerprint: no new field is added. The QUEST engine's parameters (alpha, bonferroni_family, nominal_high_card, qda_root_restriction) are already in the fingerprint. The K-dependent behaviour is captured by the model's class_labels, which already enter the fingerprint since Phase 3.1.

## §4. Session changes

### 4.1 Session.train

The engine-block for K > 2 is removed for C&RT (sub-phase 3.4.a) and QUEST (sub-phase 3.4.b). After 3.4.b, no engine is blocked by class count. The check is deleted entirely.

### 4.2 No change to fix_spec / partition / load_oot

The session pipeline is class-agnostic since Phase 3.1. The population filter of Phase 3.3 is orthogonal (it acts on rows, not on classes).

### 4.3 No change to prune / evaluate_oos / evaluate_oot

Pruning already generalises to K classes (see §0.2). The OOS/OOT evaluation path remains binary-only until Phase 3.5 (as it is today), raising `ValueError` on K > 2 as an intentional boundary.

---

## §5. Fingerprint

No new field is added. Rationale: the multiclass behaviour is a property of the model, not a configuration toggle. A model built on K=3 classes and a model built on K=2 classes with the same CSV and hyperparameters are already distinguishable by their `class_labels` and `n_classes` entries (added in Phase 3.1).

The sub-phase 3.4.b change to `quest_engine.py` — replacing QDA with a Gini sweep for K > 2 — does not change the fingerprint of a binary model. Only the multiclass case takes the new path, and the multiclass case has its own distinguishing fingerprint entries.

---

## §6. Downstream

`validation.py`, `reporting.py`, and `stability.py` remain unchanged. They continue to block K > 2 with an explicit `ValueError` pointing to Phase 3.5, as they do today. This is a documented boundary of Phase 3.4: it opens the tree building, not the metrics.

## §8. Sub-phase plan

The implementation of Phase 3.4 is divided into **two** sub-phases.

| Sub-phase | Scope | Files | Sessions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3.4.a** | C&RT multiclass: lift the block in `Session.train`. | `session_state.py`, tests | 1 |
| **3.4.b** | QUEST multiclass: extend Stage 1 and Stage 2; lift the block. | `quest_engine.py`, `session_state.py`, tests, `methodology.md` | 1 |

**Total.** 2 sessions.

**Dependency.** Strict linear order: 3.4.a first, then 3.4.b.

**Commit boundaries.** Each sub-phase ends with a commit and its `audit_log.md` entry. The phase-closure documentation (roadmap + estado_proyecto + contexto + prompt_para_asistente + design_decisions + methodology) happens in 3.4.b as a single unit.

## §9. Risks and invariants

### 9.1 Non-negotiable invariants

- **K=2 parity.** For K=2, every engine produces numerically identical trees before and after Phase 3.4. Verified by tests.
- **Fingerprint stability for K=2.** Two binary models with the same data and hyperparameters produce the same fingerprint before and after Phase 3.4.
- **No change to CHAID.** CHAID and CHAID_EXHAUSTIVE behaviour is unchanged by 3.4.
- **No change to downstream blocks.** `evaluate_oos`, `evaluate_oot`, `get_gains_table`, `get_classification_matrix` continue to raise `ValueError` for K > 2, pointing to Phase 3.5.

### 9.2 Risks

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | QUEST multiclass produces unstable trees if Stage 2 Gini sweep degenerates (constant predictor). | Low | Medium | Guard: if ΔI ≤ min_impurity_decrease for all candidate cuts, the node is a leaf. |
| 2 | K-group ANOVA F has fewer degrees of freedom, may not reach Bonferroni threshold for small samples. | Medium | Low | The threshold scales with `alpha / (2J)`, unchanged. Small samples naturally produce leaves. |
| 3 | Deviation from canonical QUEST causes confusion in documentation. | Certain | Low | Explicit "fidelity note" in §3.2 and a new `docs/methodology.md` §3.8 subsection. |
| 4 | K=2 parity broken by accidental arithmetic change in Stage 2. | Medium | High | Test `test_quest_binary_parity_after_3_4` compares every field of the produced `TreeModel` against a pre-3.4 snapshot. |

## §10. Open questions

**Q1 — C&RT and QUEST in the same phase or separate phases?**
[PROPOSED] Same phase 3.4, two sub-phases (3.4.a, 3.4.b). Rationale: C&RT is nearly free; QUEST deserves its own sub-phase. Same phase keeps the roadmap tidy.

**Q2 — Stage 1 ANOVA F extension?**
[PROPOSED] K-group F-test. Documented in §3.1.

**Q3 — Stage 2 replacement with Gini sweep?**
[PROPOSED] Gini reduction sweep for K > 2. Documented in §3.2.

**Q4 — Brown-Forsythe tie-breaker extension?**
[PROPOSED] K-group Brown-Forsythe. Documented in §3.3.

**Q5 — Retire the block entirely or leave a defensive guard?**
[PROPOSED] Retire entirely. After 3.4.b, no engine blocks K > 2. The block disappears from `Session.train`. A defensive check that no engine internally blocks K > 2 is added as a comment.

**Q6 — New `docs/methodology.md` subsection?**
[PROPOSED] Yes, add §3.8 "QUEST multiclass extensions (Phase 3.4)" with the K-group F-test formula and the Stage 2 Gini-sweep description, cited as a documented deviation from Loh & Shih (1997).
